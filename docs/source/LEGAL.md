# Legal — drafts for review, not for publication

**I am not a lawyer and this is a regulated transaction.** Everything below is a starting
draft to hand to your counsel or your broker's compliance contact. Three of the four
documents contain statements of fact about *your business* that I cannot know and must not
invent — those are marked `TK` and are the whole reason this needs a professional pass.

The one document I'd treat as urgent rather than routine is the **disclaimer**, because your
building does not exist yet and every image on the site is a rendering.

---

## 1. Disclaimer — the important one

Put this in the Legal panel and consider a short form in the footer.

> All images on this site are artist's renderings. 223 Waverly Avenue is not yet
> constructed. Renderings, floor plans, dimensions, square footages and finishes are
> illustrative, are based on plans filed with the New York City Department of Buildings and
> approved by the Landmarks Preservation Commission, and are subject to change without
> notice during design development and construction. Dimensions and square footages are
> approximate and may vary from as-built conditions. Furniture, fixtures, appliances,
> landscaping and neighbouring context shown in renderings are for illustration only and are
> not included unless expressly stated in a contract of sale. No representation is made that
> the completed building will be identical to the images shown.

**Why this matters more than the rest.** The reference carries an equivalent paragraph even
though 111 West 57th is *built*. You are marketing an unbuilt house with photorealistic
renderings — the gap between what a visitor sees and what exists is the largest legal
exposure on the site. The copy in `COPY.md` already says plainly on the contact card that
the house is not yet built; that sentence and this paragraph are doing the same job at
opposite ends of the experience, and both should stay.

**For counsel:** confirm whether this offering triggers New York offering-plan requirements
(GBL Article 23-A / Attorney General filing). A single-family house sold as a whole
generally does not, but that determination is not mine to make, and if it does, mandatory
disclosure language applies and this paragraph is nowhere near sufficient.

## 2. Standardized Operating Procedures — required if a licensed broker is involved

New York State requires licensed real estate brokers to publish their Standardized
Operating Procedures for prospective homebuyers, and to make them available on the broker's
website. **This must state your broker's actual policies. Do not publish the template.**

> Standardized Operating Procedures for Prospective Homebuyers
>
> `TK BROKERAGE NAME` has adopted the following standardized operating procedures for
> prospective homebuyers:
>
> 1. **Identification.** `TK` — state whether prospective buyers are required to show photo
>    identification, and if so at what point and why.
> 2. **Exclusive buyer representation agreement.** `TK` — state whether one is required
>    before showing a property.
> 3. **Mortgage pre-approval.** `TK` — state whether pre-approval is required, and note any
>    circumstances in which an individual seller may require it.
>
> Effective date: `TK`

Each of those three is a factual statement about how your brokerage operates. Get them from
the broker directly.

## 3. Fair Housing

Required alongside the SOP for New York brokers, and separately a good idea.

> `TK BROKERAGE NAME` fully supports the principles of the Fair Housing Act and the Equal
> Opportunity Act. We do not discriminate on the basis of race, colour, religion, sex,
> disability, familial status, national origin, sexual orientation, gender identity, age,
> marital status, military status, lawful source of income, or any other characteristic
> protected by federal, state or local law.

New York State publishes a required Fair Housing Notice and disclosure form for licensees.
**Use the official version, not this paraphrase** — your broker will have the current one,
and the wording is prescribed.

## 4. Privacy policy — blocked until the lead capture exists

This cannot be finished before you decide where inquiries go, because the policy has to
describe what the site actually collects and who receives it.

What already needs covering:

- **Analytics.** If you add GTM or any analytics — the reference runs GTM and CookieYes —
  say so, and if you have EU or UK visitors you need a consent mechanism, not just a notice.
- **Inquiry data.** `TK` — name, email, phone, message. Where does it go: an inbox, a CRM, a
  third-party form service? Each recipient is a disclosure.
- **Retention.** `TK` — how long you keep an enquiry.
- **Rights.** If you have California visitors, CCPA/CPRA rights may apply; for EU/UK, GDPR.
  A property in Brooklyn will still be browsed from both.
- **Contact for privacy requests.** `TK`

**Practical note:** if the site has no form, no analytics and no cookies — just a `mailto:`
link — the policy is short and the compliance surface is nearly nil. That's worth
considering as a deliberate choice rather than a default. The reference loads GTM and a
cookie banner; you don't have to.

## 5. Terms & conditions

Lowest risk of the four, and the most boilerplate. Cover:

- Ownership of site content and imagery, and that StudioSC retains rights in the drawings
  and renderings — **check the architect's agreement before publishing their work**, this is
  a real and commonly-missed one.
- No warranty as to accuracy; site content is not an offer or a contract.
- Limitation of liability.
- Governing law: New York.
- Contact for notices: `TK`
